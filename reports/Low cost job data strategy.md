# Build OrbitHire Around Direct Employer Feeds

**Executive recommendation.** OrbitHire should make employer-authoritative ATS feeds and structured company career pages its primary supply, use a small group of permission-compatible public APIs to fill coverage gaps, and stop treating Glassdoor, LinkedIn, or other scraped boards as dependable production inventory. The repository already contains most of the needed execution platform—Postgres task queues, eight public ATS adapters, eleven API/feed adapters, normalization, filtering, expiry, and ranking—but it fetches per user, polls only daily, lacks Workday and generic structured-site ingestion, and collapses every representation of a job into one lossy hash. The lowest-cost move is therefore **architectural, not another paid data contract**: fetch each board/query once, persist every source observation, fan the resulting canonical jobs out to users, and measure unique useful yield before buying coverage. Launch with curated Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, SmartRecruiters, Workable, Personio, Teamtailor, and Recruitee feeds; add company sitemap/`JobPosting` discovery; retain USAJOBS, Jobicy, Himalayas, Remote OK, and The Muse only under their source-specific display rules. Keep Workday behind an employer allowlist and permission/terms review, and do not scrape Glassdoor. This can hold direct API spend near zero; the clearest optional variable-cost upgrade found is Jobicy direct links at **$0.01 per newly returned job with a direct ATS URL**—at most $10 per 1,000 such new jobs—while Adzuna, Jooble, Remotive private access, and other aggregators should be purchased only after telemetry proves marginal value. ([Jobicy API](https://jobicy.com/jobs-rss-feed); [Glassdoor terms](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/terms-2022-12-01/))

The principal cost flaw is that **upstream work is scheduled per user and per search even though postings are global**. Equivalent users can therefore call the same source with the same query repeatedly, and watchlisted boards can be re-fetched once per user. A ten-minute “Fetch now” cooldown prevents one user from clicking repeatedly but does not coalesce work across users. ([cron task creation](/Users/himanshurawat/Desktop/Projects/orbithire/supabase/migrations/20250101000020_job_fetch_cron.sql:43); [manual-fetch throttle](/Users/himanshurawat/Desktop/Projects/orbithire/supabase/migrations/20250101000020_job_fetch_cron.sql:120)) No persistent run ledger measures requests, source failures, new/unique jobs, field completeness, downstream shortlists, or applications, so the system cannot yet determine which “free” feed is actually cheap per useful job.

The principal quality flaw is identity. `job_postings.dedupe_key` is a unique SHA-1 of normalized company, title, and only the first location component; it omits source, external ID, URL, requisition ID, and time. ### Workday, Glassdoor, and company sites need different answers

**Workday is valuable but should not be treated as a normal public ATS API.** Community projects consistently use the unauthenticated tenant-specific CXS route, with 20-item list pages and detail calls, but Workday does not publish this as a supported third-party vacancy API, and its site terms prohibit scraping/data mining and applications that interact with covered sites without permission. ([community route guide](https://github.com/Francis1998/agentic-career-search/blob/main/docs/guides/WORKDAY_SOURCE_GUIDE.md); [Workday site terms](https://www.workday.com/en-us/legal/site-terms.html)) The recommendation is to start from an employer’s own career link, prefer its sitemap and canonical `JobPosting` pages, and enable CXS only for a named employer after permission or terms review. If enabled, store the discovered origin, tenant, shard, site, and locale; page slowly; fetch details only for new or changed `externalPath` values; stop on `403`, `429`, CAPTCHA, or schema drift; and never probe guessed tenants or evade controls. The cleanest option for strategic employers is an employer-provided Workday outbound XML/integration feed. ([Workday Integration Cloud](https://www.workday.com/content/dam/web/en-us/documents/datasheets/workday-integration-cloud-connectors-hcml-datasheet.pdf))

**Glassdoor should not supply OrbitHire’s job text.** Its terms expressly prohibit automated agents that scrape, strip, or mine the service without written permission and restrict competitive reuse. ([Glassdoor terms](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/terms-2022-12-01/)) A Glassdoor result can be a human-facing discovery hint, but ingestion should resolve the job to the employer or ATS canonical posting. Do not fund proxies, CAPTCHA solving, or third-party datasets with undocumented Glassdoor provenance; these add cost and legal/operational risk while remaining less authoritative than the company source.

**Company websites should be a structured discovery ladder, not a universal scraper.** For each company, try: known documented ATS → `robots.txt` sitemap declarations → sitemap indexes and career-specific RSS/Atom → static detail-page `JobPosting` JSON-LD or embedded public data → an allowlisted domain adapter. Validate that structured data matches the visible single-job page, canonical employer, active `validThrough`, and working apply path. Google notes that search discovery can take days, so Google/Glassdoor search cannot substitute for direct polling. ([Google JobPosting](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)) Browser rendering is the last resort because it multiplies CPU, breakage, and anti-bot cost.

## Provenance-first storage makes freshness and quality measurable

### Recommended target architecture

OrbitHire should retain its worker and task queue but separate **acquisition**, **observations**, **canonicalization**, and **user matching**:

```text
company registry + shared search subscriptions
                    |
             fetch-plan scheduler
                    |
    ATS / sitemap / approved API adapters
                    |
       source runs + raw observations
                    |
    exact identity -> cautious entity resolution
                    |
            canonical job postings
                    |
       eligibility / quality prefilter
                    |
      fan-out matches to subscribed users
                    |
          embeddings + capped LLM rank
```

`fetch_plans` should uniquely represent a normalized board (`source`, board token, region) or query (`source`, country, normalized keyword/location set, freshness window). `source_runs` should record plan, start/end, request count, HTTP outcomes, completeness, cursor/watermark, raw/kept/unique/new/closed counts, missing-field rates, block state, and estimated cost. A scheduler leases a plan only when its source-specific TTL has expired. The current “Fetch now” behavior should return the latest sufficiently fresh shared run and enqueue a refresh only when stale. This converts request cost from roughly **users × searches × sources** to **unique plans × sources**.

The database should add `job_observations` keyed first by `(source, source_board_id, external_id)` with normalized canonical/apply URL as fallback. Each observation should contain `canonical_posting_id`, source URL, canonical URL, requisition ID, source timestamps, `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, `closed_at`, consecutive-miss count, content hash, raw payload, source-run ID, permission basis, attribution text/URL, and a snapshot-complete flag. `job_postings` remains the product’s entity, but its fields should be selected by explicit provenance precedence: employer/official record first, then a partner feed with a direct employer URL, then attributed aggregator data. `job_match_searches(match_id, search_id)` should preserve every discovery route while `job_matches` retains user status and ranking.

Deduplication should be staged. Exact identity uses stable ATS board + posting ID, normalized canonical URL, or verified requisition ID. Conservative cross-source matching can propose a merge using employer domain, normalized title, full structured location, employment type, time overlap, and description fingerprint, with a confidence score and no automatic merge for ambiguous same-title requisitions. Canonical fields should be re-electable as better observations arrive; direct employer URLs must replace redirect/aggregator URLs. This is materially safer than the current company-title-first-location hash.

Freshness needs distinct semantics: `source_published_at`, `source_updated_at`, `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, and `closed_at` must not be collapsed. When a documented complete board snapshot succeeds, mark a posting closed after two consecutive misses; do not do this for partial or capped fetches, such as the current SmartRecruiters implementation that stops after three pages and enriches only a subset. ([SmartRecruiters adapter limits](/Users/himanshurawat/Desktop/Projects/orbithire/worker/orbithire_worker/fetchers/ats_more.py:40)) Jobs with no source date should be ranked by clearly labeled discovery time with lower freshness confidence, not treated as certainly recent. Sponsorship should be an evidence-bearing enum—`explicit`, `text_inferred`, `unknown`, optionally `not_offered`—plus evidence text/source, rather than a boolean inferred from an unreliable aggregator.

Before persistence and paid ranking, quarantine records with invalid/non-HTTP apply URLs, placeholder employer/title, impossible dates, expired `validThrough`, employer mismatch, or no accessible application path. Quality scoring should reward authoritative provenance, stable identity, direct apply URL, reliable time fields, complete description, structured location/employment type, compensation evidence, and confirmed active status. The source-run ledger must connect to user outcomes—viewed, shortlisted, applied—so portfolio decisions use **unique useful jobs and applications per 1,000 requests / per dollar**, not raw result count.

## Adaptive polling cuts spend without sacrificing recency

The current once-daily global schedule is too slow for priority companies and wasteful when repeated per user. The table below is a **recommended starting policy**, not a vendor SLA; OrbitHire should tune it after a two-week measurement window.

| Source class | Starting cadence | Cost controls and closure rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Priority Greenhouse/Ashby/SmartRecruiters/Lever boards | 15–30 min | One shared list/feed request per board; conditional GET if supported; detail fetch only for new/changed records; two successful complete-snapshot misses to close. |
| Long-tail ATS boards; Teamtailor/Personio/Recruitee XML | 2–6 h; Personio no faster than hourly | Adaptive backoff for unchanged boards; fingerprint responses; respect published feed cadence. |
| Workable | Employer endpoint 1–3 h; global XML no faster than hourly | Prefer scoped endpoint; download the large global feed once per cycle, not per user/company. |
| Approved custom sitemap/JSON-LD sites | Sitemap/feed 1–6 h; details only on changed URL/`lastmod` | Static fetch first, domain budget and per-host lock, browser render only on allowlist. |
| USAJOBS | 30–60 min when relevant | Shared narrow queries, maximum useful page size, stored watermark. |
| Jobicy | 1–3 h | Documentation advises at most one new sync/hour; use cursor/status checks; attribute public results. ([Jobicy](https://jobicy.com/jobs-rss-feed)) |
| Remote OK / The Muse | 2–6 h | Shared cache, attribution, conservative rate until measured; stop on terms/schema changes. |
| Himalayas | Daily, after source refresh | Its dataset refreshes every 24 hours, so faster polling adds calls, not freshness. ([Himalayas](https://himalayas.app/docs/remote-jobs-api)) |
| Remotive, if separately justified | Daily at most | Public jobs are already delayed 24 hours; never promise them as latest. ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote-jobs/api)) |
| Adzuna/Careerjet/Reed | User-triggered or scheduled gap fill | Hard daily/monthly budgets and cache by normalized query; disable automatically near quota. |
| Jooble | Manual benchmark only | Spend its 500 lifetime free calls on controlled coverage tests. |
| HTML/JobSpy experiments | Lowest cadence, internal only | Global kill switch, robots/terms gate, strict request cap, no proxy or CAPTCHA evasion. |

Each plan should use jitter, exponential backoff, `Retry-After`, per-host concurrency, and a circuit breaker. Quiet boards can relax from 30 minutes to two, six, or twelve hours; boards that frequently post can speed up within their domain budget. Prioritize pages until reaching a stored timestamp/cursor watermark rather than assuming page one covers the desired recency window. A budget controller should cap requests/day, bytes/day, detail fetches, browser renders, and paid jobs by source, while alerts flag zero yield, schema drift, unusual duplicate rate, or blocks. The existing HTTP retry layer and scraper kill switches provide useful primitives. ([HTTP client](/Users/himanshurawat/Desktop/Projects/orbithire/worker/orbithire_worker/fetchers/http.py:1); [scraper controls](/Users/himanshurawat/Desktop/Projects/orbithire/worker/orbithire_worker/fetchers/sources/_scraper.py:1))

## The pasted research overstates free access and freshness

Several claims in the supplied memo should not enter product or financial planning. **Arbeitnow’s live job objects do not currently expose the claimed `visa_sponsorship` boolean**, even though its page documents such a filter; sponsorship must remain unverified unless evidenced in a first-party posting. ([documentation](https://blog.arbeitnow.com/blog/job-board-api); [live response](https://www.arbeitnow.com/api/job-board-api)) **Adzuna’s verified default allowance is 250/day and 2,500/month, not “a few thousand” or roughly 1,000–2,000 per day**, and ongoing commercial use can require consent/licensing. ([Adzuna terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service)) **Jooble’s free allowance is 500 lifetime requests per key**, so it is not a sustainable polling tier. ([Jooble docs](https://help.jooble.org/en/support/solutions/articles/60001448238-rest-api-documentation)) **The Muse’s current documented rate is 500 anonymous or 3,600 registered requests/hour, not about 1,000/month**, but its licence still constrains use. ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/developers/api/v2)) No current official support was found for the memo’s Careerjet 50–100/day number or the claim that Remote OK is unrestricted and hourly; both should be recorded as unknown. Remotive’s public feed is intentionally 24 hours delayed, making a 15–30-minute polling architecture pointless for that source. ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote-jobs/api))

The memo also confuses open-source software with open data rights. JobSpy is useful code, but its MIT licence does not authorize copying LinkedIn or Glassdoor content; those platforms’ terms govern the data. Similarly, publicly reachable ATS JSON does not automatically grant blanket commercial republication rights. OrbitHire should preserve the canonical application link, minimize copied content where terms are unclear, promptly honor removals, record the legal/permission basis per company/source, and obtain counsel or employer opt-in for material commercial use.

## Conclusion

OrbitHire’s durable advantage will not come from maximizing the number of free endpoints. It will come from knowing which employer boards matter to its users, observing them once at the right cadence, and preserving enough provenance to select the freshest authoritative representation without paying to rank duplicates. The current worker is a credible foundation, but the database must stop equating a fuzzy content hash with identity and the scheduler must stop equating a user search with an upstream fetch.

Once those changes are in place, paid data becomes a measurable coverage purchase instead of insurance against unknown gaps. Until then, additional aggregators mostly amplify requests, terms exposure, and duplicate noise; direct employer feeds, structured company pages, and disciplined observation telemetry produce better jobs for less money.
